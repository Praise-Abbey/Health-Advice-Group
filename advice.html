Health Advice

Extreme Cold

To stay safe during extreme cold, focus on these critical conditions:

🏠 Indoors

  • Heat key rooms: Keep your living room and bedroom at at least 18°C.
  • Close windows: Keep bedroom windows shut at night to protect your lungs.
  • Stay active: Move around at least once an hour to boost circulation.
  • Eat hot meals: Consume warm food and drinks regularly to maintain body heat.

🧥 Outdoors

  • Layer up: Wear multiple thin layers of wool or fleece instead of one thick coat.
  • Cover up: Always wear a hat, gloves, and a scarf over your mouth to warm the air you breathe.
  • Wear gripped boots: Use shoes with deep soles to avoid slipping on ice.

🚨 Emergency Warning Signs

  • Hypothermia: Watch for shivering, confusion, slurred speech, and drowsiness. Seek immediate medical help.
  • Frostbite: Watch for pale, numb, or stinging skin on fingers, toes, and ears. Warm them gently.
  • Check on others: Regularly check on elderly neighbors, infants, and vulnerable family members.

Extreme Heat

Stay safe during extreme heat by keeping hydrated, cooling your body and home, and watching for signs of heat-related illness.

💧 Hydration

  • Drink plenty of fluids: Have water, or diluted squash, regularly through the day, even if you do not feel thirsty.
  • Limit alcohol, caffeine and very sugary drinks: They can make you lose more water.
  • Eat cold meals: Salads and fruit are light and contain water.

☀️ Sun and Protection Activity

  • Stay out of the sun: Keep to the shade, especially between 11am and 3pm.
  • Dress for the heat: Wear light, loose clothes, a hat and sunscreen.
  • Take it easy: Avoid exercise or activity that makes you hotter. Plan chores for the cooler parts of the day.

🏠 Keeping your Home Cool

  • Close curtains: Shade windows that face the sun during the day.
  • Open windows at night: Let cooler air in once it is cooler outside than inside.
  • Turn off what you do not need: Lights and electrical equipment give off heat.

🚨 Emergency Warning Signs

  • Heat exhaustion: Watch for headache, dizziness, feeling sick, heavy sweating and intense thirst. Move somewhere cool, drink water and cool the skin.
  • Heatstroke: If someone stops sweating, is confused or loses consciousness, call 999 immediately.
  • Check on others: Look in on older neighbours, babies, young children and vulnerable family members.

Asthma

Asthma is a long-term condition that causes inflammation and narrowing of the airways, leading to symptoms like wheezing, coughing, and shortness of breath.

Managing it daily requires using a preventer inhaler as prescribed to reduce inflammation alongside a reliever inhaler to quickly open the airways when symptoms arise. It is also essential to attend annual NHS reviews and avoid common triggers like smoke, cold air, and pollen.

During an asthma attack, you should sit upright, take regular puffs of your reliever inhaler every few minutes, and call 999 immediately if your symptoms worsen or do not improve.

Hay fever

Hay Fever

Hay fever is a common allergy that causes sneezing, coughing and itchy eyes. You cannot cure it, but there are things you can do to help your symptoms, or medicines you can take to help.


Hay fever can be managed effectively using a combination of over-the-counter medications, barrier methods, and practical lifestyle habits


DO
  • put petroleum jelly (such as Vaseline) around your nostrils to trap pollen
  • wear wraparound sunglasses, a mask or a wide-brimmed hat to stop pollen getting into your nose and eyes
  • shower and change your clothes after you have been outside to wash pollen off
  • keep windows and doors shut as much as possible
DON'T
  • do not cut grass or walk on grass
  • do not spend too much time outside
  • do not keep fresh flowers in the house
  • do not smoke or be around smoke – it makes your symptoms worse

Home risk assessment

Tick anything that applies to your home. The advice under each item tells you what to do.

Damp and mould

Clean small patches with a suitable cleaner and improve ventilation. Mould can make asthma worse.

Open windows briefly each day and use extractor fans when cooking or bathing.

Heating and temperature

Keep the rooms you use most warm, especially in cold weather.

Close curtains in the day and open windows when it is cooler outside.

Air and allergens

Wash bedding regularly and dust with a damp cloth.

On high pollen days, keep windows closed, especially in the morning and evening.
